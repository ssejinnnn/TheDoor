01 — WHAT IS A DOOR?
1.1

THE
DOOR

A door is one of the most familiar elements of architecture, but it performs several roles at once. It creates an opening while also forming a barrier, allowing people to enter and exit while separating one space from another.

Even before a door is opened, its material, scale, color, hardware, and condition begin to shape how we understand the space behind it. A heavy metal entrance can feel protective or restricted, while a transparent glass door can appear open and accessible.

A worn wooden surface may suggest age and familiarity, while a smooth and minimal entrance can feel more controlled. The door is therefore not only a functional object. It becomes part of the identity of a building and one of the first things we read before entering.

1.2

THE
THRESHOLD

A threshold is the point where one space becomes another. It can be as physical as a step, frame, handle, or doorway, but it also represents the moment of transition between two different environments.

Before entering, we approach the door, slow down, locate the point of access, and decide whether we are allowed or invited to continue. The design of an entrance can make this transition feel gradual or immediate.

A recessed doorway may create a pause before entering, while a large glass entrance can reduce the visual distance between the street and the interior. The threshold therefore begins before the actual door is crossed. It includes the way we approach, recognize, and interact with the entrance.

1.3

INSIDE /
OUTSIDE

Every door creates a relationship between inside and outside. From the street, an entrance gives only a partial view of what exists beyond it. Glass may reveal light, movement, and activity inside, while a solid surface hides the interior completely.

The amount of visibility changes the way we experience the boundary between the two spaces. An entrance can make the interior feel connected to the street, or it can emphasize separation.

Transparency, depth, framing, and material all affect how strongly that boundary is felt. Because of this, a door does more than divide two physical spaces. It controls what can be seen, what remains hidden, and how the outside world is connected to the space within.

1.4

PUBLIC /
PRIVATE

Doors also communicate different levels of access. Some entrances are designed to welcome a large number of people. They may be wide, transparent, brightly lit, or clearly marked.

Others are smaller, heavier, or more visually closed, creating a stronger sense of privacy and restriction. These differences help us understand a space before we enter it.

A storefront, apartment building, private residence, and service entrance may all use doors differently because each one manages a different relationship between people and access. Through material, visibility, scale, and detail, a door can suggest whether a space is public, private, open, controlled, inviting, or restricted.
